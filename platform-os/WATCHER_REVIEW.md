## Overall state: **AMBER** (leaning AMBER-RED on documentation hygiene; AMBER on substance)

**Justification.** This is a genuinely strong, unusually self-critical planning set: the central existential risk (cross-tenant leak of children's data) is named, gated and tested rather than asserted; the red-teams are real adversarial work that found a live BLOCKER (F1 self-grant admin) no spec had noticed; and the sequencing debate produced a defensible adjudication (D-0) that gates *data admission* rather than *build commitment*. That is best-in-class thinking for a solo-operator project. It is **not GREEN**, because (a) the documents have not been reconciled *after* the red-team and debate — several still describe a finished state that the red-team disproved, the file-naming/cross-references are broken in places, and the v1-scope contradiction the product red-team flagged is still live; and (b) the most important live security hole (F1) is correctly *identified* but, on the evidence in these documents, has not yet been *fixed* — it remains "awaiting operator go-ahead" (D-1). A plan that contains an un-remediated, world-writable production database cannot be called GREEN regardless of how good the surrounding analysis is. AMBER is the honest state: advance only with the named, owner-assigned follow-ups below closed first.

## What genuinely got done well

- **The existential risk is gated, not asserted.** Cross-tenant leakage of children's data is named the top risk in every doc and backed by a *non-waivable, 100%-pass* isolation gate that recurs on every table-touching change (`DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md §4`, `SECURITY_GDPR_SAFEGUARDING.md §2`). This is the correct control class for the correct risk.
- **The red-team is real and earned its keep.** It found a live BLOCKER (F1 self-grant admin) that *none* of the four specs had noticed, and verified every claim against actual source files and line numbers. That is the single most valuable artefact in the set.
- **Intellectual honesty about the codebase.** DATA_MODEL flags brief-implied tables that *don't exist* (`sponsors`, `position_targets`) rather than inventing them (§3.4); the architecture grounds every claim in a cited file/symbol; TBDs and `[DPO/LEGAL]` markers are used consistently instead of fabricated figures or borrowed legal authority.
- **The sequencing debate is a model of adjudication.** Both DEBATE papers steel-man their side and concede honestly; D-0's resolution (gate the *data*, not the *build*; liability attaches at external-data admission) is a genuine synthesis, not a split-the-difference fudge.
- **Defence-in-depth is articulated correctly** — RLS as the non-bypassable spine, app-layer as a second line only, with the explicit "a leak requires all three layers to fail" statement (`SECURITY §1`). The "client filters are tripwires, not boundaries" instinct is right.
- **Governance is placed where it belongs** — a coherent BLACK-trigger list around RLS changes, bucket visibility, first external child data, and self-checkin (`DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md §6`, `SECURITY §9`), with the operator owning sign-off.
- **The commercial analysis is sober about the free incumbent** and refuses to invent numbers — the "if a club won't pay because Spond is free, the case is invalid" test is exactly the right framing, and pricing is honestly left TBD pending Track A.

## Final judgement: is this a credible, best-in-class-aspiring plan?

**Yes — credibly, and with real distinction in its risk-thinking — but it is not yet finished, and its own documents currently overstate how finished it is.**

The *substance* is among the best multi-tenant retrofit plans one could expect from a solo-operator project: the right risk is identified, gated and tested; the adversarial review is honest and found a live hole; the sequencing dilemma is adjudicated with genuine rigour rather than hand-waved. That is best-in-class *aspiration* backed by best-in-class *method* in the security and analysis layers.

What stops it being GREEN is twofold. First, **a documentation-reconciliation debt**: the specs were written in a forward pass, the red-team and debate then changed the picture, and the original documents were not all updated to match — so a reader encounters stale "GREEN", a still-live v1 scope contradiction, broken file references, and superseded-but-still-described insecure options (the GUC boundary). A hostile reader (a club committee, an ICO auditor, an investor) would seize on exactly these: "your own red-team says the live database is world-writable and your tracker says GREEN — which is it?" Second, and more seriously, **the single most important live fix (F1) is planned but, on this evidence, not yet deployed** — so the project's real-world state today is worse than any single document admits when read alone.

The honest verdict: this is a credible plan that is one disciplined editing pass and one deployed security fix away from being defensible to a hostile reader. Resolve the RED items, reconcile the documents to the post-red-team reality, and ratify (not merely recommend) the visibility line, and the state moves to GREEN. As it stands today — with a world-writable production database and a tracker still reading GREEN — the accurate state is **AMBER**, and the live site alone is **RED** until F1 ships.
